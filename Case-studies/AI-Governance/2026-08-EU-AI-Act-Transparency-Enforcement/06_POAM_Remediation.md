## Remediation Narrative
Implement a persistent, clearly visible disclosure ("You are interacting with an AI assistant") within the chatbot/agent interface itself — not buried in terms of service — satisfying Article 50(1)'s requirement that natural persons be informed they are interacting with an AI system. For deepfake or synthetic-media features, implement disclosure "upon first exposure" per Article 50(4), rendered in a clear and distinguishable manner appropriate to the content type. For generative-content outputs, implement machine-readable provenance marking using an interoperable standard such as C2PA Content Credentials, which satisfies both the EU AI Act's "machine-readable mark" requirement and aligns with California SB 942's AI-detection-tool and disclosure provisions, avoiding duplicate jurisdiction-specific engineering work. Coordinate with Legal to ensure the compliance attestation references both regimes accurately and is kept current as enforcement guidance develops.

## Compensating Controls
Until technical disclosure/watermarking features ship, publish an interim public statement (e.g., in product documentation or terms of service) disclosing that the platform's chatbot and content-generation features are AI-powered, as a good-faith interim measure while the in-product UI work is completed — this does not fully satisfy Article 50's "clear and distinguishable" in-context disclosure standard but reduces exposure during the implementation window.

## Verification & Closure Criteria
Closure requires: (1) production deployment of conspicuous AI-interaction disclosure in the chatbot interface, verified by product QA and screenshot evidence retained in the case file; (2) production deployment of machine-readable watermarking on generative outputs, verified against the chosen standard's validation tooling; (3) Legal-approved compliance attestation published to the customer trust center. Evidence should be retained to support both EU AI Act Article 50 and California SB 942 compliance reviews.
